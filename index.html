Builder Journey

Builder Journey

My path into product didn’t begin with software.

It began in a restaurant business, solving practical problems under real pressure.

01
↗ Stage 1

Operations First

While building Yume Ramen, I used AI to handle practical operational work faster.

That meant clearer tools for:

  • Food safety documentation
  • Allergen information
  • Pricing tools
  • Operational workflows
  • Process documentation

It showed me how technology could make daily execution cleaner.

02
⟳ Stage 2

From Operations To Tools

Documentation work led to sharper product questions:

Can this process be automated?

Can this become a tool?

Could software solve this problem more effectively?

That was the shift from operator to builder.

03
◇ Stage 3

Building Something I Wanted To Use

Meditation and mental performance had been personal interests for years, but I wanted more structure.

So I started building the tool I wanted to use.

This became VEXIS.

Along the way I learned:

  • GitHub workflows
  • Product design
  • Deployment
  • Audio systems
  • Testing and feedback
  • Development workflows
04
✓ Stage 4

Learning Through Shipping

Shipping introduced new constraints.

Features broke. Audio failed. Deployments failed. Bugs appeared. Fixes created new bugs.

I learned by diagnosing, fixing, testing, and shipping again.

Most of my current skills came from that process.

Flagship Case Study

VEXIS

ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT

Mental Performance Platform

VEXIS is a mental performance platform built around Awareness → Intuition → Execution.

It connects structured practice, reflection, progress, and a longer-term vision for personalised training.

Explore VEXIS

Origin

Why VEXIS Exists

In 2020, a period of anxiety and stress pushed me to take mindfulness and mental training seriously.

What began as a way to regain calm became a long-term interest in how people perform under pressure.

I studied meditation, performance habits, and training methods used by high performers.

Years later, while building Yume Ramen, that interest turned into a product question:

Could mental training become more accessible?

That question became the foundation of VEXIS.

First version

How It Started

The first version was simple: guided audio practices for foundational mental training. No progression system or SignAI concept yet — just a focused tool to build from.

Product evolution

How VEXIS Evolved

01

Guided Audio Practices

VEXIS moved from static instructions into guided sessions with focus, playback, progress, and reflection.

02

Structured Training

A clearer training map grouped practices into foundation, intuition, and flow.

03

Progress Tracking

Progress tracking added accountability through sessions, minutes, streaks, completion rate, and history.

04

Expanded Practice Library

Completion and next-practice flows helped users finish, repeat, or continue training.

Flagship Case Study

Sentient Mountain Monk

ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT

Entertainment-First Companion

Sentient Mountain Monk began inside VEXIS, then became a standalone companion concept built around humour, emotional interaction, and personality.

It is a small example of product discovery: noticing an unexpected signal and giving it a sharper shape.

Enter the Mountain

Unexpected discovery

How It Started

Sentient Mountain Monk began during VEXIS development.

Some experimental responses started drifting away from VEXIS.

They sounded less like a training guide and more like a calm, slightly absurd mountain monk.

The personality was distinct enough to test as its own concept.

Unexpected Discovery

During VEXIS development, experiments revealed a different personality.

Instead of forcing it back into VEXIS, I treated it as a product signal.

The result became a standalone concept built around humour, emotional interaction, and personality design.

Product question

The Question

Many products focus on productivity, coaching, or information.

I became interested in a simpler question:

What if people didn’t need another productivity tool? What if they simply needed somewhere to vent?

This idea eventually became:

“Spill Your Tea To The Monk.”

A simple interaction: share a frustration, story, or everyday problem; get a response from a fictional mountain monk.

Product evolution

How The Idea Evolved

01

02

Creates a simple, memorable product identity.

The shift from guidance to conversation and entertainment.

Early personality and response design.

Product decisions

Key Product Decisions

Why A Monk?

The monk creates distance, helping users laugh at situations instead of sitting inside them.

Why Entertainment First?

Entertainment lowers the barrier to engagement.

Why “Spill Your Tea”?

The action is instantly understandable.

Behaviour design

Designing The Behaviour

The experience is not defined by the model alone.

Behaviour comes from product choices, constraints, and tone.

Personality rules Product decisions Interaction design Tone guidelines Behaviour frameworks

The goal is not more responses.

The goal is a consistent experience.
